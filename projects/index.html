Memory Response Paths and Path-Structured Context Compilation

AAAI under review

Joint first author, submitted to AAAI and under review (2026)

  • With Jerrold Huang, I study how a single memory changes an LLM agent's decisions. I proposed Memory Response Paths to estimate its incremental contribution across four horizons.
  • Designed paired memory-availability tests across 180 environments, along with model API workflows, statistical analyses, and context ablations that hold evidence fixed.
  • In 56.1% of environments, identical final responses concealed differences in earlier response paths.
  • In a separate 144-item MemGym-DR evaluation, PSCC raised LLM-judged quality by 6.9 percentage points and used 32.8% fewer API tokens than the benchmark's A-Mem baseline.

Sequential Tone Residualization and Abnormal Returns in Earnings Calls

ICAIF 2026 oral

Joint first author, accepted at ICAIF 2026 and selected for oral presentation

  • With Jerrold Huang, I study whether the tone of earnings-call answers carries information for investment research after accounting for financial and dialogue context.
  • Proposed the application and dictionary and LLM validation, co-designed the information sets for tone residualization, and aligned Capital IQ, IBES, and CRSP data by event time.
  • Across 1,901 held-out 2025 calls, a one-standard-deviation increase in the answer-tone residual was associated with a 61.5 basis point increase in one-day market-adjusted returns. The standardization scale came from the development sample.
  • Tone prediction achieved positive out-of-sample R². Prespecified joint pre-event placebo tests found no significant association.

Age-Biased Technological Change

Sole RA, UBC Sauder

Research assistant at UBC Sauder (Apr 2026–Present)

  • As the sole research assistant, I link CPS, O*NET, and USPTO data to measure technological exposure, harmonizing more than 60 historical O*NET releases.
  • Validate PatentsBERTa measures against Qwen 3 and manually review a sample of records.
  • Construct variables, select fixed effects, and fit weighted fixed-effects OLS and GMM models of wages and occupational sorting.
  • Run estimation, bootstrap inference, and robustness checks in R and Python on HPC/Slurm. Maintain the replication package and draft the empirical section.

The project is led by J. Frank Li and Cheng Chen at UBC Sauder.
